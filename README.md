## Parafree Lie rings and associative algebras in Lean 4

This repository formalizes Theorems A, B, C, and D of **The Parafree Conjecture
for Lie rings and associative algebras**, by Vasily Ionin and Roman Mikhailov.
It constructs the specific Lie rings and associative algebras in the manuscript
and proves the stated parafreeness, homology, finite-presentation, and transfinite
length assertions.

The main statements are restated as short, compiled examples in
[Parafree/MainResults.lean](Parafree/MainResults.lean). The public import is
`Parafree`; each theorem also has a focused import. The proofs use mathlib and
[TimmPeterson/lie-algebras](https://github.com/TimmPeterson/lie-algebras), pinned
to the revisions recorded below.

### Build

With [elan](https://github.com/leanprover/elan) installed, run:

```sh
git clone https://github.com/madoka-wizard/parafree-lie-associative.git
cd parafree-lie-associative
lake exe cache get
lake build
```

The checked-in `lean-toolchain` selects Lean **4.29.0**. The manifest pins the
complete dependency graph; `lake update` is not needed for a reproducible build.
The cache command downloads the available mathlib build artifacts. Lake builds
the remaining dependencies and this project's proofs.

For the full theorem and axiom checks:

```sh
lake build Parafree.Audit.Main Parafree.Audit.Upstream
lake env lean Parafree/Audit/Main.lean
lake env lean Parafree/Audit/Upstream.lean
```

The last two commands run the guards in fresh Lean processes, even when Lake
reuses previously built audit modules. On Windows with PowerShell 7, the
repository also provides:

```powershell
.\scripts\lake.ps1 build
.\scripts\check.ps1
```

`check.ps1` additionally scans every project source for proof placeholders,
rejects missing project imports, and verifies that every Lean source belongs
to the audited import closure. It prints a compact summary and writes complete
logs under `tmp/check/`. Use `-ShowLog` to display complete build output. The
Windows wrapper handles Git's tar utility, process-local dependency trust, and
an optional local elan installation; it does not change the global toolchain.

### Main proven results

The homology of a Lie ring is the actual Chevalley–Eilenberg homology from
LieRings. For an augmented associative algebra, homology is the left derived
coinvariant functor evaluated on the trivial module, with its natural module
structure over the coefficient ring. Thus it computes the usual Tor with
trivial coefficients.

All four final theorems are unconditional for the specified objects. In C and
D the coefficient ring is an **arbitrary nonzero commutative ring**: there is
no field, domain, Noetherian, or characteristic assumption.

#### Theorem A

Let

$$
L_0=\left\langle q_1,q_2,q_3,z,u,v\;\middle|\;
z=[u,v]+2q_1,\quad u=[v,z]+2q_2,\quad v=2q_3-[z,u]\right\rangle
$$

be the integral Lie ring with these three relators, and let
$L=L_0/\gamma_\omega(L_0)$ be its residual quotient. Then:

- $L$ is parafree of rank three, with the para-equivalence from the free Lie
  ring on $q_1,q_2,q_3$ sending each generator to its indicated class in $L$.
- Its second homology satisfies

$$
H_2(L;\mathbb Z)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb F_2\cong\mathbb F_2.
$$

The endpoint is [`Parafree.TheoremA.theoremA`](Parafree/TheoremA/Complete.lean).
`homologyTwoTensorEquiv` puts the field factor first and gives a field-linear
isomorphism. `homologyTwoTensorRightEquiv` gives the manuscript's tensor order.
The proof identifies the residual quotient with a divided-relator subring of
a rational free Lie algebra, compares its reduction with a graded Lie algebra,
and computes actual second homology using the split-extension comparison.

#### Theorem B

In the rational free Lie algebra on $t,a$, put

$$
w=[t,a]-2a,\qquad
f_1=[t,[t,w]]-w,\qquad
f_2=[a,[t,[t,a]]]-[w,[w,[t,w]]].
$$

Let $P$ be the integral Lie subring generated by $t,a,f_1/5,f_2/5$. Then:

- $P$ is parafree of rank two. The specified para-equivalence sends the free
  generators to $t$ and $a-2f_1/5$.
- Its second homology satisfies

$$
H_2(P;\mathbb Z)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb F_5
\cong\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb N}\mathbb F_5.
$$

- $P$ is finitely generated and is not finitely presented.

The endpoint is [`Parafree.TheoremB.theoremB`](Parafree/TheoremB/Complete.lean).
The model is the actual rational Lie subring, with the displayed right-nested
brackets. Finite generation is witnessed by the surjective free Lie map on the
four specified generators. Finite presentation means finitely many free
Lie generators and finitely many Lie-ideal relators.

The proof constructs the required reduction and coefficient maps through a
localized Magnus representation. The infinite homology calculation and the
finite-presentation obstruction are proved for this concrete model.

#### Theorem C

For a nonzero commutative ring $k$, define

$$
e_n=(1+a)(1+b)^n(1+c)-(1+d)(1+x)^n(1+y),\qquad n\in\mathbb N,
$$

and give all six generators augmentation zero. Let

$$
A=k\langle a,b,c,d,x,y\rangle/(e_n\mid n\in\mathbb N),
$$

where the relation ideal is two-sided. Then:

- $A$ is residually nilpotent and parafree of rank four, with the specified
  para-equivalence sending the free generators to $a,b,c,d$.
- There is an isomorphism of $k$-modules

$$
H_2(A)=\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_2(k,k)
\cong\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb N}k.
$$

- $A$ is finitely generated and is not finitely presented as a unital
  associative $k$-algebra.

The endpoint is [`Parafree.TheoremC.theoremC`](Parafree/TheoremC/Complete.lean).
The countable direct sum is represented by `ℕ →₀ k`. The finite-presentation
predicate allows any finite free-algebra presentation, not just a finite
subset of the displayed infinite family.

The formal proof computes the homology directly from a projective resolution
of length two. A faithful embedding into a free algebra proves independence
of the relevant word family and injectivity of the second differential.
After taking coinvariants, the basis vector for the $n$th relation maps to
$v+n w$, where

$$
v=E_a+E_c-E_d-E_y,\qquad w=E_b-E_x.
$$

The second kernel has the explicit basis

$$
E_{n+2}+(n+1)E_0-(n+2)E_1,\qquad n\in\mathbb N.
$$

A separate relation-coordinate argument proves failure of finite presentation
over every nonzero commutative ring. A reusable Tietze lemma makes the argument
independent of the chosen finite generating presentation.

#### Theorem D

Over the same coefficient ring, let

$$
A_0=k\langle a,b,c,d,x,y\rangle/(e_0,e_1),\qquad I=\ker(\varepsilon_{A_0}).
$$

Define the transfinite augmentation series by

$$
I^0=A_0,\qquad I^{\alpha+1}=I^\alpha I+II^\alpha,\qquad
I^\lambda=\bigcap_{\beta<\lambda}I^\beta
\quad\text{for nonzero limit ordinals }\lambda.
$$

Its least stationary ordinal is exactly $\omega^2$:

$$
I^{\omega^2}=I^{\omega^2+1},\qquad
I^\alpha\ne I^{\alpha+1}\quad(\alpha<\omega^2).
$$

The endpoint is [`Parafree.TheoremD.theoremD`](Parafree/TheoremD/Complete.lean).
The proof identifies the first limit with the kernel of $A_0\to A$, relates
successive omega-multiple stages to powers of that kernel, and combines a
normal-word filtration with explicit nonzero witnesses and separation.
Both the stabilization and its minimality are included in the endpoint.

| The transfinite sequence in D | `TheoremD.ordinalPower` | [OrdinalPowers](Parafree/TheoremD/OrdinalPowers.lean) |

The declarations in this table lie under the namespace `Parafree`.
Mathlib indexes the lower central series from zero: its term with index zero
is the whole Lie algebra. Taking the intersection over all these terms agrees
with the conventional positive-indexed residual ideal. Associative
para-equivalences are checked at every natural power; the extra zeroth
quotient is the trivial quotient and does not change the definition.

For example:

```lean
import Parafree.TheoremC.Complete

open Parafree.TheoremC

example (k : Type*) [CommRing k] [Nontrivial k] :
    Nonempty (homology k 2 ≃ₗ[k] (ℕ →₀ k)) :=
  (theoremC k).2.2.1
```

The more complete examples in [MainResults.lean](Parafree/MainResults.lean)
are compiled as part of the default build.

### Project layout

```text
Parafree.lean                  public import
Parafree/MainResults.lean      compiled overview of Theorems A–D
Parafree/TheoremA/             integral presentation, divided model, and mod-two homology
Parafree/TheoremB/             rational subring, Magnus maps, and mod-five homology
Parafree/TheoremC/             associative presentations, parafreeness, resolution, and Tor
Parafree/TheoremD/             normal-word filtration and exact transfinite length
Parafree/Lie/                  reusable Lie-algebra and scalar-reduction constructions
Parafree/Associative/          augmentations, free derivatives, PBW tools, and coinvariants
Parafree/Linear/               bases, tensor products, exterior powers, and filtrations
Parafree/Homology/             coefficient CE complexes and homological comparisons
Parafree/Upstream/Koszul/      licensed compatibility ports of four mathlib modules
Parafree/Audit/                literal statement checks and transitive axiom audits
scripts/                      Windows build and whole-project verification wrappers
```

The theorem directories use shared supporting results; they do not duplicate
independent copies of the same general constructions. In particular, C and D
share their associative presentations and embeddings. Each theorem has a
`Complete.lean` endpoint and its own import wrapper.

### Verification and trust

There are no `sorry`, `admit`, or additional axiom declarations in the project.
The [statement audits](Parafree/Audit/Main.lean) independently check the literal
presentations, canonical maps, coefficient rings, homology definitions,
finite-presentation conventions, and transfinite recursion.

The axiom checker follows declaration types and proof bodies transitively.
It covers declarations by their source module, including private helpers and
the compatibility ports, and accepts only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and
`Quot.sound`. The [upstream audit](Parafree/Audit/Upstream.lean) separately
checks the generic PBW theorem, free-Lie second-homology vanishing, the Hopf
comparison, and its naturality.

The full check rejects orphaned source modules and missing local imports, so
stale compiled files cannot substitute for absent project sources. Dependency
linter messages are kept in the build log; they are separate from the
project's source and axiom checks.
